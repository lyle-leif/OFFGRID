## Before typhoon season
Know your area's hazards: flooding, landslides, and storm surge along the coast. Find out where your barangay's designated evacuation center is and walk the route in daylight. Prepare a go-bag for each family member and agree on a family meeting place and a contact person outside your area.

## Understanding warnings
PAGASA issues Tropical Cyclone Wind Signals (TCWS) numbered 1 to 5 for specific areas. A higher number means stronger winds and less warning time. Signal No. 1: winds of 39 to 61 km/h expected within 36 hours, minimal to minor threat to lives and properties. Signal No. 2: 62 to 88 km/h within 24 hours, minor to moderate threat. Signal No. 3: 89 to 117 km/h within 18 hours, moderate to significant threat. Signal No. 4: 118 to 184 km/h within 12 hours, significant to severe threat. Signal No. 5: 185 km/h or higher within 12 hours, extreme threat to lives and properties. (Source: DOST-PAGASA press release on the modified TCWS system, 23 March 2022.) Listen for whether your province or municipality is included, and follow PAGASA, NDRRMC, and your local government on radio or TV. Saved information on this device cannot tell you the current signal level.

## When a typhoon is approaching
Charge phones and power banks, fill containers with clean water, and stock food that does not need cooking. Secure or bring inside loose objects that strong winds can throw, such as roofing sheets, plants, and signs. Trim weak tree branches near the house if it is safe to do so. Move important items and electrical appliances to higher shelves in case of flooding.

## When to evacuate
Evacuate immediately when local authorities order it, especially if you live in a low-lying, coastal, riverside, or landslide-prone area. Do not wait for water to rise. Bring your go-bag, turn off the main power switch and gas if you can do so safely, and lock your house.

## During the typhoon
Stay indoors and away from windows and glass doors. Stay in the strongest part of the house. Do not go outside during the calm "eye" of the storm because strong winds return suddenly from the opposite direction. Keep listening to a battery-powered radio for updates.

## After the typhoon
Wait for authorities to say it is safe before returning home. Watch for fallen power lines, damaged structures, and floodwater. Do not touch electrical lines. Check your house for damage before entering. Boil drinking water or use treated water if the supply may be contaminated.
