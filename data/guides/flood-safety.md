## Warning signs of flooding
Heavy rain for many hours, rapidly rising rivers or creeks, and water entering streets are signs of possible flooding. Flash floods can arrive within minutes, especially near rivers and in low areas. If authorities issue a flood or rainfall warning for your area, prepare to move to higher ground.

## Walking or driving through floodwater
Avoid walking or driving through floodwater. Just 15 centimeters (6 inches) of fast-moving water can knock an adult down, and about 60 centimeters (2 feet) can carry away a car. Floodwater can hide open manholes, sharp debris, and live electrical wires. If you must walk through water, use a stick to check the ground in front of you and walk where the water is not moving.

## Electrical safety in floods
Turn off the main power switch before water enters the house, but only if you are standing on dry ground. Never touch electrical appliances, outlets, or switches while you are wet or standing in water. Stay away from fallen power lines and report them.

## Health risks after floods
Floodwater is often contaminated with sewage and animal urine. Wading through it can cause leptospirosis, a serious infection that enters through cuts and the eyes, nose, or mouth. Wear boots if you must go through floodwater, wash with soap and clean water afterward, and see a health worker if you develop symptoms such as fever, muscle pain, headache, or red eyes in the following days.

## Clean drinking water
Do not drink floodwater or water from flooded wells. Use bottled water, or boil water at a rolling boil for at least one minute before drinking. Throw away food that touched floodwater.

## If you are trapped by rising water
Move to the highest floor or the roof. Do not enter a closed attic without a way out. Signal for help with a whistle, flashlight, or brightly colored cloth. Call emergency services (911 in the Philippines) if you have a signal.
