## Before helping anyone
<!-- status: draft -->
Check that the area is safe for you before approaching an injured person. Call emergency services (911 in the Philippines) for serious injuries. Wear gloves or use a plastic bag as a barrier if available when touching blood.

## Bleeding
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: Department of Health | url: https://doh.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/OMNIBUS-HEALTH-GUIDELINES-FOR-ADOLESCENTS-2022.pdf -->
Stop bleeding by pressing firmly and directly on the wound with a clean cloth, gauze or bandage, and keep pressing. Wear gloves or clean your hands first if you can. If blood soaks through, add more cloth on top instead of removing the first layer. Go to a health facility if the wound is deep or large, if the bleeding does not stop, or if it is a puncture wound such as from a nail.

## Cuts and wounds
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: Department of Health | url: https://doh.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/OMNIBUS-HEALTH-GUIDELINES-FOR-ADOLESCENTS-2022.pdf -->
Once a small cut stops bleeding, rinse it under clean running water, wash it with soap and water, and cover it with a clean gauze pad or adhesive bandage. See a health worker if the wound is deep or large, keeps bleeding, or has a high risk of infection, such as a puncture from a nail or a wound from floodwater or an animal bite.

## Burns
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: Department of Health | url: https://doh.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/OMNIBUS-HEALTH-GUIDELINES-FOR-CHILDREN-2022.pdf -->
First stop the burning: move the person away from the heat, or remove the heat source. Cool the burn with cool or cold running water for at least 10 minutes. Do not put ice directly on it, and do not use toothpaste, butter or oil. Do not break blisters. Cover the burn loosely with a clean, sterile dressing. Get medical help if the burn is deep or large; if it is on the hands, feet, face, groin or head; if it goes all the way around an arm or leg; or if it shows signs of infection.

## Possible broken bones
<!-- status: draft -->
Do not try to straighten the injured part. Keep it still with a splint made from a board, rolled newspaper, or cardboard, padded with cloth and tied above and below the injury. Seek medical care.

## Dehydration and heat exhaustion
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: Department of Health | url: https://doh.gov.ph/press-release/doh-warns-against-heat-stroke/ -->
Heat exhaustion can start with tiredness, dizziness, headache, vomiting, weakness or feeling faint. Move the person to a cool, shaded place with good air flow and remove outer clothing. Cool them with cold compresses or cold wet cloths on the head, neck, armpits, wrists, ankles and groin. If they are awake, give frequent small sips of cool water. Heat stroke is an emergency: very high body temperature, a fast heartbeat, confusion, seizures or unconsciousness. Start cooling right away and get the person to a hospital or call 911.

## Unresponsive person
<!-- status: draft -->
If a person does not respond and is not breathing normally, call 911 and start CPR if you are trained: push hard and fast in the center of the chest, about 100 to 120 times a minute. If the person is breathing, turn them on their side in the recovery position and stay with them until help arrives.
