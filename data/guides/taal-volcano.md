## Taal Volcano alert levels 0 to 2
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: DOST-PHIVOLCS | url: https://www.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/index.php/volcano-hazard/volcano-alert-level -->
PHIVOLCS uses alert levels 0 to 5 for Taal Volcano. Alert Level 0 (normal): no major eruption is expected, but steam and gas explosions can still happen without warning, and no one should live permanently on Taal Volcano Island. Alert Level 1 (low-level unrest): steam or gas explosions can happen without warning; entry into the Main Crater, the Daang Kastila fissure area and the Mt. Tabaro eruption site is not allowed. Alert Level 2 (increasing unrest): explosions are more likely; entry into Taal Volcano Island is not allowed, and communities in the highest-hazard areas must prepare for possible evacuation. Saved information on this device cannot tell you the current alert level: follow PHIVOLCS and your municipal DRRM office.

## Taal Volcano alert levels 3 to 5: evacuation
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: DOST-PHIVOLCS | url: https://www.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/index.php/volcano-hazard/volcano-alert-level -->
Alert Level 3 (magmatic unrest): an eruption is imminent; Taal Volcano Island, Taal Lake and the pre-identified Batangas lakeshore communities facing the active vent must be evacuated. Alert Level 4 (hazardous eruption imminent): a strong eruption is underway and could become violently explosive; communities in the pre-determined hazard zones must be evacuated. Alert Level 5 (highly hazardous eruption in progress): a violent eruption is ongoing, and more areas may need to evacuate depending on conditions. If you live in these areas, follow your local government's evacuation orders.

## Dangers during a Taal eruption
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: DOST-PHIVOLCS | url: https://www.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/index.php/volcano-hazard/volcano-alert-level -->
A violent eruption of Taal can bring base surges and pyroclastic density currents (fast, hot clouds of gas and rock that sweep across the lake and land), a volcanic tsunami on Taal Lake, thick ashfall, ground cracks, lahars (volcanic mudflows) and landslides. These are why PHIVOLCS asks people to leave the island, the lake and the lakeshore when the alert level rises.

## Ashfall: protecting your health
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: Department of Health | url: https://doh.gov.ph/doh-press-release/doh-warns-public-on-taal-eruptive-activity -->
During ashfall, stay indoors and limit outdoor activities. Close windows and doors, and turn off air conditioners and fans so ash does not get inside. If you must go out, wear an N95 mask, protective clothing and goggles. People with asthma or other breathing problems should use an N95 mask; others may use a dust or surgical mask. Ash can irritate the nose, throat and eyes and cause coughing or difficult breathing: anyone with these symptoms should go to the nearest health facility.

## Ashfall on the skin
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: Department of Health (Negros Island Region) | url: https://nir.doh.gov.ph/protektahan-ang-balat-laban-sa-ashfall/ -->
Cover your skin when you go out during ashfall. If ash gets on your skin, wash it off promptly with clean water and mild soap, and do not scratch itchy skin.
