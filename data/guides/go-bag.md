## What a go-bag is
A go-bag (emergency bag) is a ready-packed bag you can grab immediately when you must evacuate. Prepare one per person, keep it light enough to carry while walking, and store it near the door. Check it every few months and replace expired food, water, and medicine.

## Water and food
Pack drinking water, about 3 to 4 liters per person per day for at least 3 days if you can carry it, or as much as you can carry plus a way to get more. Pack ready-to-eat food that does not need cooking or much water: canned goods with pull-tab lids, crackers, biscuits, peanut butter, and energy bars. Include a manual can opener if cans need one.

## Documents and money
Put copies of important documents in a waterproof plastic bag or envelope: valid IDs, birth certificates, insurance papers, land titles, and medical records or prescriptions. Bring some cash in small bills and coins because ATMs and card payments may not work during a brownout. Write down important phone numbers on paper in case your phone battery dies.

## Light, power, and communication
Pack a flashlight and extra batteries, a whistle to signal for help, a fully charged power bank and charging cable, and a battery-powered or hand-crank radio to hear official announcements. Avoid using candles inside crowded evacuation areas because of fire risk.

## Medicine and first aid
Pack a basic first aid kit: bandages, gauze, adhesive tape, antiseptic, alcohol or hand sanitizer, and pain or fever medicine. Bring at least a one-week supply of maintenance medicines, with a copy of the prescription. Include face masks, soap, and personal hygiene items.

## Clothes and protection
Pack a change of clothes, a raincoat or poncho, a light blanket or emergency foil blanket, sturdy closed shoes or boots, and a cap or hat. Rubber boots help in flooded areas.

## Infants and young children
For infants, pack diapers, wipes, baby formula or breast-milk storage supplies, clean bottles, safe drinking water for mixing formula, extra baby clothes, a blanket, and any baby medicines. Bring a familiar small toy or comfort item for young children. Keep a recent photo of each child and write your contact number on a card in their pocket in case you get separated.

## Older adults, persons with disabilities, and pets
Pack extra maintenance medicine, eyeglasses, hearing-aid batteries, mobility aids, and adult diapers if needed. Write down medical conditions and doctor contacts. For pets, pack food, water, a leash or carrier, and vaccination records; ask in advance whether your evacuation site accepts pets.
