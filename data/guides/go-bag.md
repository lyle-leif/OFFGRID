## What a go-bag is
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: Office of Civil Defense | url: https://pia.gov.ph/disaster-information-service/civil-defense-promotes-go-bag-for-emergencies/ -->
A go-bag (emergency bag) is a ready-packed bag you can grab immediately when you must evacuate, with what your family needs for at least 3 days. Keep it where you can reach it quickly and light enough to carry while walking. Check it every three months and replace expired food, water and medicine.

## Water and food
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: Office of Civil Defense | url: https://pia.gov.ph/disaster-information-service/civil-defense-promotes-go-bag-for-emergencies/ -->
Pack bottled drinking water for each person, and ready-to-eat food, for at least 3 days: food that does not need cooking, such as canned goods with pull-tab lids, crackers, biscuits and energy bars. Include a manual can opener if your cans need one.

## Documents and money
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: Office of Civil Defense | url: https://pia.gov.ph/disaster-information-service/civil-defense-promotes-go-bag-for-emergencies/ -->
Put important documents in a sealed, waterproof plastic bag: valid IDs, birth certificates, land titles, insurance papers, and medical records or prescriptions. Bring cash in small bills and coins, because ATMs and card payments may not work during a brownout. Write important phone numbers on paper in case your phone battery dies.

## Light, power, and communication
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: Office of Civil Defense | url: https://pia.gov.ph/disaster-information-service/civil-defense-promotes-go-bag-for-emergencies/ -->
Pack a flashlight, a whistle to signal for help, matches, a portable radio with spare batteries to hear official announcements, and your mobile phone with a charged power bank and cable. If you bring a candle, never leave it burning unattended, especially in crowded evacuation areas.

## Medicine and first aid
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: Office of Civil Defense | url: https://pia.gov.ph/disaster-information-service/civil-defense-promotes-go-bag-for-emergencies/ -->
Pack a first aid kit with bandages, gauze and medicine for fever, diarrhea and wounds. Bring your regular maintenance medicines with a copy of the prescription, plus soap, face masks, and sanitary and personal hygiene items.

## Clothes and protection
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: Office of Civil Defense | url: https://pia.gov.ph/disaster-information-service/civil-defense-promotes-go-bag-for-emergencies/ -->
Pack a change of clothes, rain gear such as a raincoat or poncho, and a blanket or sleeping bag. Sturdy closed shoes or boots help in flooded or damaged areas. A rope can also be useful.

## Infants and young children
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: Office of Civil Defense | url: https://pia.gov.ph/disaster-information-service/civil-defense-promotes-go-bag-for-emergencies/ -->
For infants and babies, bring diapers, wipes, baby formula or breast-milk storage supplies, clean bottles, safe drinking water for mixing formula, extra baby clothes, a blanket and any baby medicines. For young children, bring a familiar small toy or comfort item. Write your contact number on a card in each child's pocket in case you get separated.

## Older adults, persons with disabilities, and pets
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: Office of Civil Defense | url: https://pia.gov.ph/disaster-information-service/civil-defense-promotes-go-bag-for-emergencies/ -->
Add what older adults and persons with disabilities need: extra maintenance medicine, eyeglasses, hearing-aid batteries, mobility aids and adult diapers. Write down medical conditions and doctor contacts. For pets, pack food, water, a leash or carrier and vaccination records, and ask in advance whether your evacuation site accepts pets.
