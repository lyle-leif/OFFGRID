## Preparing for brownouts
<!-- status: draft -->
Keep flashlights and spare batteries where everyone can find them in the dark. Keep phones and power banks charged when power is available. Know how to reach your electric cooperative or utility to report outages.

## Lighting safely
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: Department of Energy | url: https://doe.gov.ph/consumer-connect/fire-safety-tips-home-0 -->
Flashlights and battery LED lamps are safer than candles or kerosene lamps. If you must use a candle or oil lamp, put it in a proper, stable holder, away from curtains and where children or pets cannot knock it over. Never leave it burning unattended, and put it out before you sleep.

## Generators and carbon monoxide
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: Department of Health (via Philippine News Agency) | url: https://www.pna.gov.ph/articles/1121873 -->
A generator used the wrong way can fill a house, garage or building with carbon monoxide, a gas you cannot see or smell that can kill. Never run a generator indoors or in a closed garage; keep it outdoors and away from windows and doors. Signs of carbon monoxide poisoning include headache, dizziness, nausea, confusion, shortness of breath and feeling weak or sick: move to fresh air immediately and seek medical help.

## Appliances and power surges
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: Bureau of Fire Protection (via Philippine Information Agency) | url: https://pia.gov.ph/news/ensuring-fire-safety-is-everybodys-responsibility-bfp-reminds/ -->
Unplug appliances you are not using, especially during an outage, so they are not damaged when power comes back. Do not overload outlets or extension cords, and replace frayed cords right away. Leave one light switched on so you know when the power is back.

## Food safety without refrigeration
<!-- status: draft -->
Keep the refrigerator and freezer doors closed as much as possible. A closed refrigerator keeps food cold for about 4 hours. Throw away perishable food such as meat, fish, eggs, and leftovers that have been above refrigerator temperature for more than 2 hours or that smell or look unusual.

## Saving phone battery
<!-- status: draft -->
Lower screen brightness, turn on battery saver, close unused apps, and turn off mobile data and Wi-Fi when you do not need them. Send text messages instead of calling when networks are congested, because texts need less network capacity.

## Heat during outages
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: Department of Health | url: https://doh.gov.ph/press-release/doh-warns-against-heat-stroke/ -->
Without fans or air conditioning, drink plenty of water and avoid soda, coffee, iced tea and alcohol. Wear loose, light clothing, stay in the coolest shaded part of the house, and limit time outdoors between 10 am and 4 pm. Check on infants, young children, older adults and sick people, who are more affected by heat.
