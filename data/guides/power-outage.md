## Preparing for brownouts
Keep flashlights and spare batteries where everyone can find them in the dark. Keep phones and power banks charged when power is available. Know how to reach your electric cooperative or utility to report outages.

## Lighting safely
Use flashlights or battery LED lamps instead of candles or kerosene lamps when possible. If you must use a candle, put it in a stable holder away from curtains and children, and never leave it burning while you sleep.

## Generators and carbon monoxide
Never run a generator, charcoal stove, or gas grill indoors or in a closed garage. They produce carbon monoxide, a gas you cannot see or smell that can kill. Keep generators outdoors and away from windows and doors. Signs of carbon monoxide poisoning include headache, dizziness, nausea, and confusion; move to fresh air immediately and seek medical help.

## Appliances and power surges
Unplug sensitive appliances such as computers and TVs during an outage to protect them from surges when power returns. Leave one light switched on so you know when power is back. Do not overload extension cords when power returns.

## Food safety without refrigeration
Keep the refrigerator and freezer doors closed as much as possible. A closed refrigerator keeps food cold for about 4 hours. Throw away perishable food such as meat, fish, eggs, and leftovers that have been above refrigerator temperature for more than 2 hours or that smell or look unusual.

## Saving phone battery
Lower screen brightness, turn on battery saver, close unused apps, and turn off mobile data and Wi-Fi when you do not need them. Send text messages instead of calling when networks are congested, because texts need less network capacity.

## Heat during outages
Without fans or air conditioning, drink water often, wear light clothing, and rest in the coolest shaded part of the house. Check on older adults, infants, and sick people, who are more affected by heat.
