## During an earthquake: drop, cover and hold
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: DOST-PHIVOLCS | url: https://www.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/press-release-primer-on-the-30-september-2025-magnitude-mw-6-9-offshore-northern-cebu-earthquake/ -->
When you feel strong shaking, protect yourself right away: drop to the ground, take cover (for example under a sturdy table) and hold on until the shaking stops. This is the "drop, cover and hold" that PHIVOLCS asks everyone to do during a strongly felt earthquake.

## After an earthquake: aftershocks and damaged buildings
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: DOST-PHIVOLCS | url: https://www.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/press-release-primer-on-the-30-september-2025-magnitude-mw-6-9-offshore-northern-cebu-earthquake/ -->
Expect aftershocks for several days to weeks after a strong earthquake. Be careful around buildings that look damaged, because aftershocks can weaken them further. Ask your city or municipal engineering office to inspect a damaged building, and do not go back inside until a structural engineer says it is safe. Check slopes near your home for new cracks: cracked slopes can slide, especially during heavy rain or aftershocks, so stay away from them. Get information only from PHIVOLCS and your local DRRM office, and do not share messages from unconfirmed sources.

## Preparing your home for earthquakes
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: DOST-PHIVOLCS | url: https://www.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/press-release-primer-on-the-30-september-2025-magnitude-mw-6-9-offshore-northern-cebu-earthquake/ -->
Prepare before an earthquake happens. Fasten heavy furniture and appliances to the walls, and secure hanging objects so they cannot fall on anyone during shaking. Practice drop, cover and hold with your family.

## Tsunami warning signs: shake, drop, roar
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: DOST-PHIVOLCS | url: https://www.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/press-release-are-you-ready-know-what-to-do-in-case-of-a-tsunami/ -->
A tsunami caused by a nearby earthquake can reach the coast within minutes, before any official warning. Learn the three natural signs: strong ground shaking (shake), a sudden drop or change in the sea level (drop), and a roaring sound of incoming waves (roar). If you notice any one of these near the coast, move to high ground immediately and stay away from beaches, rivers and other waterways. After a strong earthquake nearby, stay alert and keep away from the shore.

## Earthquake intensity: what the numbers mean
<!-- checked: 2026-10-27 | source: DOST-PHIVOLCS | url: https://www.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/phivolcs-earthquake-intensity-scale-peis/ -->
PHIVOLCS reports how strongly an earthquake was felt in each place using the PHIVOLCS Earthquake Intensity Scale (PEIS), from I to X. Intensity I to III: felt by few or many people indoors, with hanging objects swinging. Intensity IV (moderately strong): most people indoors feel it and dishes and windows rattle. Intensity V (strong): most people feel it and some run outdoors. Intensity VI (very strong): many people are frightened, some lose their balance and weak buildings get slight damage. Intensity VII (destructive): people struggle to stand on upper floors and weak or poorly built structures are badly damaged. Intensity VIII to X (very destructive to completely devastating): people cannot stand even outdoors, many or most buildings are damaged or destroyed, and landslides and ground cracks occur.
